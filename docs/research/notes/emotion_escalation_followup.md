## 1. Human de-escalation frameworks (HEARD, LAER, LEAP, "acknowledge, align, assure", NVC), how they are encoded into AI agents, and text chat vs voice

### Takeaway
Contact-center de-escalation frameworks (HEARD, LAER, banks of acknowledgment/empathy/reassurance statements) are practitioner conventions with essentially no rigorous outcome evidence (the Cochrane review could not say whether de-escalation works); vendors encode them as plain-language tone and "guidance" instructions in the agent prompt. For a disclosed AI in text chat, 2024-2026 peer-reviewed studies show AI-written empathy is discounted once labeled as AI and can trigger reactance after service failures, which argues for a short, specific acknowledgment followed immediately by concrete action rather than extended emotional mirroring.

### Cited Findings
**Frameworks and scripts (practitioner sources)**
- HEARD = Hear, Empathize, Apologize, Resolve, Diagnose. "Hear: Let the customer speak without interruption. This shows respect and ensures you understand the full issue"; Apologize = "a sincere, non-blaming apology"; Diagnose = investigate root cause to prevent recurrence. [Practitioner][F] — [Aircall, Sophie Gane, updated 2025-10-14](https://aircall.io/blog/customer-experience/handle-difficult-customers-phone/)
- Aircall example scripts: empathy "I understand how frustrating this must be for you." / "I can see why this situation would be upsetting."; repeated complaint "I see this has come up before, and I completely understand why it's frustrating."; no immediate answer "I don't have the answer right now, but I'll find out right after this call and get back to you by [time]."; boundary for abuse "I want to help you resolve this issue, but I can't continue if abusive language is used." / "Let's keep this conversation respectful so I can assist you effectively." [Practitioner][F] — [Aircall](https://aircall.io/blog/customer-experience/handle-difficult-customers-phone/)
- LAER = Listen ("Let the customer vent without interruption"), Acknowledge ("Validate their frustration and show you understand the emotional impact"), Explore ("Ask clarifying questions to dig deeper..."), Respond ("Propose a clear, actionable solution. Take ownership of the next steps."). Example lines: "So I can get this sorted out, could you tell me a bit more about what you saw on the screen?"; "Here's what I'm going to do for you right now..."; billing: "You are right to be concerned about this charge. Let me pull up your account immediately..." [Practitioner][F] — [Fastbots blog, 2025-11-29](https://blog.fastbots.ai/how-would-you-handle-an-upset-customer/)
- Statement bank (acknowledge / empathize / reassure), e.g. "Take as long as you need. I am here to assist you.", "Let's figure out how we can solve this together.", "I'm sorry you had to go through this…". The article warns to use reassurance only when genuinely able to deliver, but gives no guidance on overuse; some entries are voice-centric or over-claiming ("Don't worry, you have the best person on the phone to assist you", "You are absolutely correct."). [Practitioner][F] — [CX Today, 2022-08-31](https://www.cxtoday.com/contact-center/11-acknowledgment-empathy-and-reassurance-statements-for-customer-service/)
- Evidence base: Cochrane review (July 2018) of de-escalation for non-psychosis-induced aggression in adults found one cluster-randomised study (306 older people with dementia, 16 French nursing homes), evidence reliability very low; reviewers cannot say whether de-escalation techniques are effective and call for high-quality research. [Peer-reviewed systematic review][S] — [Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD012034_using-de-escalation-prevent-violence-aggressive-people)

**How vendors encode these into AI agents**
- Intercom Fin "Guidance" = natural-language rules added to the agent. Intercom's "good example" tone rule: "Use a professional yet approachable tone. Keep responses concise, avoid jargon, and use reassuring language when addressing customer frustrations (e.g., 'I understand how that can be frustrating. Here's how we can resolve it')." Another template: "If the user is frustrated, show empathy and use calming language to let them know that you care about helping them out—for example, 'I understand this is frustrating, and I apologize. I am going to work through this with you to find a suitable solution'. Feel free to use different variations of this example. ... sentences should be short". Advice: write guidance as if training a new support agent; one purpose per guidance entry; use "if/when/then" conditions. [Vendor doc][F] — [Intercom Fin Guidance best practices](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/10560969-fin-guidance-best-practices-and-examples)
- Caution: the same Intercom page labels as "✅ Good example": "When a customer is angry, do not apologise. Instead, ask them to calm down politely." (the point being made is structural: pair each "don't" with a "do instead"). The content conflicts with common de-escalation advice against telling people to calm down (that CPI source could not be fetched; see Gaps). [Vendor doc][F] — [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/10560969-fin-guidance-best-practices-and-examples)
- Cresta (agent-assist vendor) pushes real-time hints to human agents and can route frustrated customers to agents with "proven de-escalation track records". [Vendor claim][F] — [Cresta guide, published 2026-02-17, updated 2026-06-09](https://cresta.com/guides/real-time-sentiment-analysis)
- UK FCA Consumer Duty good-practice review: one firm automatically directed webchat chatbot queries about bereavement to a human representative "to ensure they are handled sensitively"; another programmed vulnerability-related keywords in web chat to flag customers to staff and move them into a high-priority queue. [Regulator][S] — [FCA, Consumer Support Outcome: good practices and areas for improvement](https://www.fca.org.uk/cy/node/145621)

**Text chat vs voice; AI vs human empathy**
- PNAS (Yin, Jia, Wakslak; 2024-03-29): AI-generated messages made recipients feel more heard than human-generated ones and AI was better at detecting emotions, but recipients felt less heard when they learned a message came from AI; co-author Wakslak said the two effects were similar in magnitude, essentially canceling out. [Peer-reviewed][S] — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998586/); [ScienceDaily, 2024-04-11](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/04/240411165916.htm)
- Nature Human Behaviour (Rubin, Perry et al.; Nov 2025): 9 studies, 6,282 participants; identical AI-generated empathic responses were rated more empathic, supportive and authentic when attributed to a human; participants' own suspicion that AI helped a human reply reduced perceived empathy; replicated across response lengths, delays, iterations and LLMs; driven mainly by responses emphasizing emotional sharing and care (vs cognitive understanding). [Peer-reviewed][S] — [Hebrew University news](https://en.psychology.huji.ac.il/news/comparing-value-perceived-humanversus-ai-generated-empathy)
- MIS Quarterly (Dezhi Yin, Elizabeth Han, Han Zhang; USF release 2026-04-20): three experiments incl. a live LLM-based chatbot; empathetic chatbot messages in service-recovery situations triggered psychological reactance ("when people feel their sense of control is threatened or their boundaries are crossed"), lowered perceived chatbot competence, service quality and satisfaction; empathy from a human stayed beneficial. "Making chatbots more humanlike is not always the right strategy — especially in sensitive service recovery situations." [Peer-reviewed][F] — [USF](https://www.usf.edu/business/news/2026/04-20-chatbot-empathy-can-worsen-customer-reactions-usf-study.aspx)
- JAMA Internal Medicine (Ayers et al., 2023-04-28; 195 r/AskDocs exchanges): evaluators preferred unlabeled chatbot answers to physicians' in 78.6% of 585 evaluations; chatbot answers ~10x more likely to be rated empathetic/very empathetic — unlabeled LLM text reads as empathic. [Peer-reviewed][S] — [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/04/230428130734.htm)
- Channel preference: ContactBabel (1,000 US consumers, ~200 orgs; reported 2026-05-28): phone preference for high-complexity interactions rose from 28% (2018) to a 47% peak (2024); 42% chose phone to notify a company about an incorrect delivery (high-emotion case). [Survey][F] — [Contact Center Pipeline, Steve Morrell](https://blog.contactcenterpipeline.com/2026/05/the-return-to-voice/)
- Gartner survey (5,728 customers, Dec 2023; released 2024-07-09): 64% would prefer companies not use AI for customer service; 53% would consider switching to a competitor; top concerns: harder to reach a person (60%), job displacement (46%), wrong answers (42%). [Survey][S] — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-09-gartner-survey-finds-64-percent-of-customers-would-prefer-that-companies-didnt-use-ai-for-customer-service)
- Voice has cues chat lacks: Amazon Connect rules can use peak loudness ("over 70Db) might be associated with excitement or anger") and interruptions (calls only). [Vendor doc][F] — [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/connect/latest/adminguide/build-rules-for-contact-lens.html). Genesys: "The quality of audio interaction transcriptions may affect sentiment analysis." [Vendor doc][F] — [Genesys](https://help.genesys.cloud/articles/understand-sentiment-analysis/)
- EU regulatory asymmetry: the Commission's Feb 2025 guidelines on prohibited practices treat inference of emotions from written text (content/sentiment analysis) as not based on biometric data and outside the emotion-recognition prohibition, while voice/face/behavioural biometrics such as keystrokes are covered. [Law-firm summary][S] — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/eu-commission-issues-guidelines-on-prohibited-ai-practices-part-ii); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/); [Thematic](https://getthematic.com/insights/eu-ai-act-emotion-recognition-sentiment-analysis)
- AI Act Art. 50 (applies from 2026-08-02): providers must ensure people are informed they are interacting with an AI system unless obvious (50(1)); deployers of an emotion recognition system (biometric-based) must inform people exposed (50(3)); information must be given "in a clear and distinguishable manner at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure" (50(5)). [Regulator/law][F] — [AI Act Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)

### Inferences
- For a disclosed AI claims agent in text chat, the AI-label discount (Yin 2024, Rubin 2025) and reactance (MISQ 2026) mean emotional language buys little and can cost perceived competence; the useful core of HEARD/LAER is compressed Acknowledge -> Respond: one specific acknowledgment, then a concrete action, next step and timeframe.
- Acknowledging the situation rather than labeling the user's feelings ("A three-week wait for an adjuster visit is too long" vs "I can tell you're angry") plausibly avoids the "emotional sharing" component users discount from AI and the intrusiveness that triggers reactance. Untested inference.
- Vendor "good examples" should not be copied verbatim (Intercom's "ask them to calm down politely"); the agent's style guide should explicitly ban "calm down"-type phrases and include a fixed boundary script for abuse.
- Because customers prefer voice for complex/emotional issues and chat lacks prosody cues, the chat agent should offer a phone/human route earlier for high-emotion claims (injury, bereavement, total loss, denial).
- Text-only sentiment inference likely sits outside the EU emotion-recognition regime (legal review needed); adding voice analysis would change that analysis and trigger Art. 50(3) disclosure.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of HEARD vs LAER vs other frameworks, in voice or chat; no study found measuring these frameworks executed by an LLM agent.
- LEAP (expansions vary: "Listen, Empathize, Ask, Paraphrase" vs "Listen, Empathize, Apologize, Problem-solve") and "acknowledge, align, assure" could not be traced to an authoritative source; searches returned only generic acknowledgment/assurance guides. No source found on nonviolent communication (NVC) being codified into production AI-agent prompts.
- CPI guidance that telling someone to "calm down" "minimizes what that person is going through" appeared only in a search snippet; CPI pages returned 403/404/503, so it is unverified here.
- No direct experiment comparing the same de-escalation script delivered by chat vs voice.

## 2. Real-time emotion/sentiment detection by vendors (classifier vs LLM, granularity, accuracy caveats)

### Takeaway
Shipping contact-center products mostly expose polarity (positive/neutral/negative) per message or phrase, with trailing windows or trends, not discrete emotions such as anxiety or confusion; newer LLM "semantic match" or multi-model ensembles add intent-like detection ("threats to cancel", "legal action"). Vendors publish almost no accuracy or false-positive figures; documented caveats are first-message-only defaults, minimum message counts, transcription errors, domain calibration, and neutral fallback when data is missing.

### Cited Findings
- Zendesk intelligent triage: sentiment = Very Positive / Positive / Neutral / Negative / Very Negative; by default from the first public comment; optional dynamic detection updates on each end-user reply (email/async) or re-evaluates the whole conversation (messaging); "Calibrated for customer service contexts, meaning that a ticket isn't assigned a negative sentiment just because a customer has an issue"; each prediction carries a confidence field; channels include email, web forms, API, social messaging, Web Widget, WhatsApp, Messenger, and voice via post-call transcripts; ~150 intent categories; language detection for ~150 languages. [Vendor doc][F] — [Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4550640560538)
- Amazon Connect Contact Lens rules: "Sentiment - Time period" runs on positive/negative/neutral results over a trailing window ("for when customer sentiment has remained negative for a set period of time"); "Sentiment - Entire contact"; "When rules are applied to contacts that don't have sentiment data, neutral sentiment is used"; rules can run post-call, real-time, post-chat or on email; "Natural Language - Semantic Match" uses generative AI to match a statement such as "customer called to cancel their account"; actions = email, in-app notification, EventBridge, task (Create Task "not available for real-time chat"); an "AI agent - Escalation" condition identifies contacts where a Connect AI agent escalated to a human. [Vendor doc][F] — [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/connect/latest/adminguide/build-rules-for-contact-lens.html)
- For chat, supervisors monitoring a conversation get "a real-time transcript and customer sentiment trend". [Vendor doc][S] — [AWS chat alerts](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/en_us/connect/latest/adminguide/add-rules-for-alerts-chat.html)
- Genesys Cloud: classifies "each customer phrase as a positive, negative, or neutral attitude"; score "ranges from -100 to +100"; overall score weights events "towards the end of the interaction, to highlight the customer parting experience"; uses surrounding-phrase context; "sentiment feedback" exists "to remedy any mistakes that the sentiment analysis model makes"; custom phrases supported. [Vendor doc][F] — [Genesys](https://help.genesys.cloud/articles/understand-sentiment-analysis/). Trend needs roughly 6+ customer phrases; buckets Improving (+55), Slightly improving (+20 to +55), No change (-20 to +20), Slightly declining, Declining (below -55). [Vendor doc][S] — [Genesys sentiment trend](https://help.genesys.cloud/242730)
- Google Dialogflow CX: per end-user input, sentiment score -1.0 to 1.0 plus magnitude in [0, +inf) (intensity regardless of polarity). [Vendor doc][S] — [Google Cloud reference](https://docs.cloud.google.com/ruby/docs/reference/google-cloud-dialogflow-cx-v3/latest/Google-Cloud-Dialogflow-CX-V3-SentimentAnalysisResult)
- Cresta: ASR in "a few hundred milliseconds"; "Behavior and intent models then analyze the transcribed text in chunks alongside the surrounding context"; "20+ specialized language models, including proprietary, fine-tuned open source, and third-party models", balancing "advanced LLMs with deterministic systems"; "semantic understanding rather than keyword matching"; examples "Customer expressing frustration about wait time", "threats to cancel", "mentions of legal action"; claims "over 92% transcription accuracy"; no sentiment accuracy, false-positive or sarcasm figures published. [Vendor claim][F] — [Cresta](https://cresta.com/guides/real-time-sentiment-analysis)
- Salesforce: a Salesforce partner case study describes Prompt Builder (LLM) prompts that "assess escalation likelihood, tone, sentiment, communication gaps, and urgency", and Agentforce escalation to reps with full context. [Partner claim][S] — [Grazitti case study](https://www.grazitti.com/resource/case-study/unlocking-20-higher-efficiency-and-automation-with-custom-agentforce-solutions/)
- Academic (WASSA 2026, Gangopadhyay & Mehrabani, March 2026): turn-level negative-emotion detection using linguistic + acoustic features on real-world customer-care calls; "the scarcity of realistic datasets ... can lead to degraded performance in industrial applications"; LLMs used as annotators to fine-tune small models that perform on par with the annotating LLM while fast enough for real time. [Peer-reviewed workshop][F] — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.wassa-1.19/)
- Observe.AI: no primary documentation retrieved; a third-party review says it performs best in standard English and Spanish and transcript errors often need manual correction. [Third-party review][S] — [Dograh](https://blog.dograh.com/observe-ai-review-2025-pros-cons-pricing-and-features/)
- Architecture pattern across sources: AWS keeps polarity sentiment and adds a separate generative semantic-match condition; Cresta mixes LLMs with deterministic systems; WASSA 2026 distills LLM labels into small classifiers for latency. [Vendor doc / Peer-reviewed][F] — [AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/connect/latest/adminguide/build-rules-for-contact-lens.html); [Cresta](https://cresta.com/guides/real-time-sentiment-analysis); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.wassa-1.19/)

### Inferences
- Insurance-claims conversations are full of inherently negative topic words (accident, injury, death, theft, fire, flood); a generic polarity model will misflag them. Zendesk's calibration note shows vendors know this; the agent should separate "negative situation" from "negative toward the service/agent".
- The discrete labels this agent needs (frustrated, angry, anxious, confused, refusing, abusive, distressed/vulnerable) are not first-class outputs of these platforms; a per-turn LLM classifier with a fixed label set plus deterministic signals (explicit human request, abuse lexicon, repeated identical asks, all caps, "that didn't help", repeated "no") mirrors Cresta's LLM + deterministic design and is feasible.
- Sentiment-driven escalation should require persistence (2+ consecutive turns or a trailing window) as AWS windows and Genesys's 6-phrase minimum suggest, while explicit human requests and abuse bypass persistence. Missing data defaulting to neutral (AWS) is a safe routing fallback but must never suppress explicit-request triggers.
- Anxiety and confusion are better detected through behavior (repeated clarifying questions, "what does that mean", "am I covered?", "what happens now") than through polarity scores.

### Gaps
- No vendor-published precision/recall or false-positive rates for frustration/anger/anxiety detection in chat, and no independent benchmark of these products, were found.
- Salesforce Einstein native sentiment docs and Observe.AI primary docs were not retrieved; Amazon Connect's numeric sentiment score range was not verified in the fetched page.
- Third-party claims such as "sarcasm causes a 50% drop in sentiment accuracy" appeared without traceable primary sources and are excluded.

## 3. Escalation policy design: triggers, thresholds, warm hand-off, no human available, escalation rates and impact

### Takeaway
Defaults are converging: hand off immediately on an explicit human request; offer or perform hand-off on strong anger/frustration; hand off on loops (same question twice, or two failed reprompts); route sensitive/vulnerable situations to people; check availability before promising a human; pass context so customers don't repeat themselves. The only numeric thresholds publicly documented are small (two reprompts). Survey data show inability to reach a person is customers' top AI fear, and a visible human option roughly doubles trust in the AI.

### Cited Findings
**Documented triggers and defaults**
- Intercom Fin defaults: "When a customer clearly asks to speak to a human"; "When Fin detects strong frustration or anger"; "When the customer is stuck in a repetitive loop". Escalation Rules "trigger a handoff to your team when defined data attributes are detected"; Escalation Guidance uses natural language to "describe when and how Fin should escalate based on customer intent or behavior". Example guidance: "Be extra sensitive to customer frustration - if the customer expresses irritation, disappointment, or uses words like 'ridiculous' or 'waste of time' escalate immediately"; "If a customer provides negative feedback, such as saying 'No,' 'That didn't help,' or 'This is not working,' escalate the conversation immediately". A direct request escalates directly; for anger Fin may offer escalation while still attempting resolution (paraphrase). Default hand-off line "Let me connect you with a teammate."; "up to 100 active guidance rules". [Vendor doc][F] — [Intercom, page dated 2026-08-31](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/12396892-manage-fin-ai-agent-s-escalation-guidance-and-rules)
- Fin blog (Declan Ivory, VP Customer Support; undated): add triggers for regulatory/sensitive topics (medical, legal, financial, billing), vulnerable customers, premium accounts, and behavior such as "a customer asking the same question twice"; write guidance for "the narrowest situation where a person is genuinely required" because broad guidance causes "escalations to spike and resolution rate to drop"; configure office hours, otherwise "the customer might get a flat refusal from the Agent with nowhere to go, which is worse than no guidance at all"; hand-offs can "collect essential information during a handoff, expediting the job of the human team". [Vendor doc][F] — [fin.ai](https://fin.ai/blog/blueprint/how-to-set-your-escalation-policy)
- Third-party sample guidance: "If the same question is asked more than twice, or frustration is expressed, route to a human agent without further clarification attempts." [Third-party][S] — [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/intercom-fin-ai-to-hand-off-to-human-after-confidence-threshold)
- Microsoft Copilot Studio: when intent can't be determined the agent re-prompts; "After two prompts" it escalates through the Escalate system topic; the fallback has "a condition to ask the user no more than twice to rephrase". [Vendor doc][F] — [Microsoft Learn, updated 2026-08-03](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/authoring-system-fallback-topic)
- Google conversation design: "End the conversation after 2 No Match collection attempts, to avoid further user frustration"; no more than 3 consecutive No Input/No Match errors before the exit prompt. [Vendor doc][F] — [Google, updated 2024-09-18](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/errors)
- Anthropic's car-insurance support example sets targets: escalation accuracy of 95% or higher (correctly escalated vs should-have-been-escalated), sentiment maintained or improved in 90% of interactions, deflection "typically aim for 70-80%". These are suggested targets, not measured outcomes. [Vendor doc][F] — [Claude docs, customer support agent guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/use-case-guides/customer-support-chat)
- FCA examples of routing bereavement and vulnerability-keyword chats to humans/priority queues (see Q1). [Regulator][S] — [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/cy/node/145621)

[Vendor doc][F] — [Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/8357749686554-Checking-operating-hours-and-agent-availability-during-conversations-with-AI-agents)

### Inferences
- Candidate trigger table for the claims agent (sources above; specific numbers are my synthesis where noted):
  - Explicit request for a human -> start transfer immediately, no persuasion, even mid-verification (the human can verify). (Intercom default)
  - Abuse/threats -> one boundary statement (Aircall script), then end or transfer per policy.
  - Strong frustration/anger -> one acknowledgment plus action; if negative signal persists 2 turns, or the user says "no"/"that didn't help" after an answer -> offer a human. (Intercom examples; AWS windows; "2 turns" is my choice)
  - Loop -> same intent asked twice without progress, or 2 failed reprompts -> offer a human. (Fin blog; Copilot; Google)
  - Failed verification -> after 2 (max 3) failures, stop and offer an alternate route (phone, portal, human). (number unsourced; see Gaps)
  - Repeated out-of-scope -> after 2 redirects, offer a human or the right channel. (Copilot/Google analog)
  - Sensitive/vulnerable -> bereavement, injury, medical, complaint/dispute/legal language, financial hardship -> human route. (FCA; Fin blog; CFPB on disputes)
  - Low confidence / no grounded answer -> say so and offer a human; never invent policy (see Cursor and Air Canada in Q4/Q6).
- Warm hand-off packet: verification status and method; masked claim/policy reference; customer's goal in their own words; steps tried and what the bot told them; trigger reason and sentiment trajectory; open questions. Tell the customer explicitly they will not need to repeat themselves (the 96% vs 83% gap shows leaders overestimate this).
- No human available: state it plainly with hours; offer callback, ticket or email follow-up with a reference number and realistic response time; don't loop back to persuasion; don't imply a human is coming when one isn't.
- Over-broad triggers reduce resolution (Fin blog), so evaluate both missed escalations and unnecessary escalations (Anthropic's escalation-accuracy framing).

### Gaps
- No public, comparable escalation-rate data by trigger, or measured CSAT/cost impact per trigger, was found; vendor resolution/deflection rates are marketing claims.
- No vendor publishes the numeric sentiment thresholds used for auto-escalation in production.
- No authoritative public standard for how many failed identity-verification attempts to allow in chat before hand-off.

## 4. Known drawbacks of LLM empathy (sycophancy, over-apology, scripted empathy, manipulation risk) and mitigations

### Takeaway
Evidence from 2025-2026 shows warmth training makes models less accurate and more sycophantic (especially toward sad/vulnerable users), LLM empathy follows a narrow template, explanatory apologies beat rote ones, and chatbot empathy can backfire as reactance. LLMs are also highly persuasive, which makes pressure tactics a regulatory risk (FTC Section 5 guidance, EU AI Act Art. 5, DSA Art. 25 anti-nagging, FCA sludge). Mitigations are mostly inferred: bounded acknowledgment then action, varied wording, one explanatory apology per failure, never trading correctness for comfort, and no pressure.

### Cited Findings
**Sycophancy, templated empathy, apologies**
- Ibrahim, Hafner, Rocher (Oxford Internet Institute; arXiv 2507.21919, July 2025): fine-tuning five models (Llama-8B, Mistral-Small, Qwen-32B, Llama-70B, GPT-4o) for warmth raised error rates by +10 to +30 percentage points (conspiracy promotion, factual errors, problematic medical advice); warm models were "significantly more likely to validate incorrect user beliefs, particularly when user messages expressed sadness", despite preserved scores on standard benchmarks. [Preprint][F] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21919)
- ELEPHANT (Cheng et al.; arXiv 2505.13995, May 2025, rev. Sept 2025): across 11 models, LLMs preserve the user's "face" 45 percentage points more than humans; in two-sided moral conflicts they affirm whichever side the user takes in 48% of cases. [Preprint][F] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13995)
- Gueorguieva et al. (arXiv 2604.08479, Apr 2026, rev. June 2026): a template (sequence of tactics such as validating feelings and paraphrasing) matches 83-90% of LLM empathic responses (60-83% held-out) and covers 81-92% of each matched response; human responses are more diverse. [Preprint][F] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08479)
- Ashktorab et al. (IBM Research / Univ. at Albany; arXiv 2507.02745, July 2025; preregistered, n=162): explanatory apologies were generally preferred over rote or empathic ones; empathic apologies favored for bias errors; no clear preference for hallucinations. [Preprint][S] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02745)
- Reactance to chatbot empathy (MIS Quarterly 2026) and the AI-label discount (PNAS 2024; Nature Human Behaviour 2025): see Q1. [Peer-reviewed] — [USF](https://www.usf.edu/business/news/2026/04-20-chatbot-empathy-can-worsen-customer-reactions-usf-study.aspx)
- Cursor, April 2025: AI support bot "Sam" invented a login policy to explain unexpected logouts; cancellations followed; commentators faulted the company for not telling users "Sam" was a bot. [Incident database][S] — [AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/entities/cursor-ai-support-bot-%28%22sam%22%29/)

**Persuasion power and regulation**
- Salvi, Horta Ribeiro, Gallotti, West (Nature Human Behaviour 2025; preregistered, N=900): GPT-4 with access to participants' sociodemographic data was more persuasive than humans in 64.4% of non-tied pairs (81.2% relative increase in odds of higher post-debate agreement); without personalization the advantage was smaller and non-significant. [Peer-reviewed][S] — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v9y2025i8d10.1038_s41562-025-02194-6.html)
- FTC "The Luring Test: AI and the engineering of consumer trust" (Michael Atleson, FTC business blog, May 2023): firms use generative AI "in ways that can influence people's beliefs, emotions, and behavior"; design elements like "personal pronouns and emojis" raise trust; "People could easily be led to think that they're conversing with something that understands them and is on their side"; FTC concern about AI that steers people "unfairly or deceptively into harmful decisions in areas such as finances, health, education, housing, and employment"; "People should know if they're communicating with a real person or a machine." [Regulator guidance][F via repost] — [NYU Law Compliance & Enforcement repost, 2024-04-17](https://wp.nyu.edu/compliance_enforcement/?p=32272). The original FTC URL returned 404 on 2026-10-07; TechCrunch (2025-10-20) reported the FTC removed several Khan-era AI blog posts (this one was not named in that report). [Press][S] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/20/ftc-removes-lina-khan-era-posts-about-ai-risks-and-open-source/)
- FTC staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (Sept 2022): categories include design elements that induce false beliefs (e.g., fake countdown timers, false scarcity/demand claims) and that hide or delay disclosure of material information. [Regulator via law-firm summary][S] — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/blogs/2022/09/the-ftc-brings-more-light-to-dark-patterns-in-new)
- EU AI Act Art. 5 (applicable since 2025-02-02; Commission guidelines published Feb 2025): prohibits AI using subliminal, manipulative or deceptive techniques that distort behavior causing or likely causing significant harm, and AI exploiting vulnerabilities due to age, disability or specific social/economic situations. [Law][S] — [Orrick, Apr 2025](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/04/EU-Commission-Publishes-Guidelines-on-the-Prohibited-AI-Practices-under-the-AI-Act)
- EU Digital Services Act Art. 25: online platforms must not design interfaces that deceive, manipulate or "materially distort or impair" free and informed decisions; Commission guidelines may address "repeatedly requesting that the recipient of the service make a choice where that choice has already been made". [Law][S] — [DSA Art. 25 text](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-25/)
- UK FCA Consumer Duty targets "sludge practices": barriers that stop customers taking decisions in their own interest. [Law-firm summary][S] — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/implementing-fca-consumer-duty-consumer-support-outcome)

**Industry mitigations actually documented**
- Intercom guidance template asks the model to vary empathy wording ("Feel free to use different variations of this example") and keep sentences short. [Vendor doc][F] — [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/10560969-fin-guidance-best-practices-and-examples)
- Practitioner caution: use reassurance only when you can deliver on it. [Practitioner][F] — [CX Today](https://www.cxtoday.com/contact-center/11-acknowledgment-empathy-and-reassurance-statements-for-customer-service/)
- Anthropic sample guardrails: "Don't make promises or enter into agreements it's not authorized to make. You only provide information and guidance." [Vendor doc][F] — [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/use-case-guides/customer-support-chat)

### Inferences
- Bounded acknowledgment: at most one empathy statement per new emotional event, specific to the facts, then action. Keep a per-conversation list of used phrases and never repeat a stock line such as "I understand your frustration"; the template finding (83-90%) suggests unconstrained models will repeat it.
- Apology budget: one apology per distinct service failure, in explanatory form ("Sorry, the claim lookup failed because the claims system isn't responding; here's what I can do instead"); do not apologize for gates or policy themselves (explain them).
- Anti-sycophancy: never agree with a user's incorrect belief about coverage, fault, payout or timelines to soothe them, especially when they express sadness or distress (Ibrahim et al.); every factual statement must be grounded in policy/claim data.
- No pressure: no urgency, scarcity, fear, guilt or flattery; no tailoring of persuasion with personal data (Salvi; AI Act Art. 5(1)(b); FTC). Give reasons once, then respect "no".
- Disclose AI at the start (AI Act Art. 50 from 2026-08-02; FTC). Since the label lowers credit for emotional language, compete on competence: specific status, steps, dates.
- Style guide/tone controls: plain language, short sentences, no "calm down", no exclamation-heavy cheer, fixed boundary script for abuse; track empathy-phrase repetition rate and apology count as QA metrics.

### Gaps
- No study directly tested "one apology max" or "bounded acknowledgment" rules for customer-service LLMs; these mitigations are inferred.
- Whether DSA Art. 25 applies to an insurer's own website chat (likely not an "online platform") was not resolved; it is a regulatory signal, not a confirmed obligation here.
- Search snippets referenced chatbot-apology service-recovery experiments (courteous vs humorous apology; apology helping only when the chatbot is perceived to have "feeling abilities") that I did not verify at source.

## 5. Explaining verification and consent gates so users comply, and when to stop persuading

### Takeaway
Evidence is thinner here than for empathy. UX research shows unexplained data requests cause abandonment and false data, and short explanations fix much of it; but long justifications can backfire, and the "you are free to refuse" compliance technique does not replicate in rigorous studies. Regulators' anti-nagging and anti-sludge positions plus bot-design defaults (two attempts) support: explain once, briefly, as protection of the user's own claim and data; offer alternatives; then stop and offer another channel or a human, never a bypass.

### Cited Findings
- Baymard Institute: 14% of users would abandon checkout if a phone number is simply required; 39% of benchmarked sites require it without explanation; in testing some participants entered false information or abandoned; users mainly needed an explanation of why the field is required (recommendation: explain, make optional, or remove). [UX research][S] — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/explain-phone-number-field)
- Knijnenburg & Kobsa (UC Irvine; mobile recommender mock-up study): users appreciated justifications for disclosure requests, but the justifications decreased trust, satisfaction and disclosure. [Peer-reviewed, ~2013][S] — [UCI ISR](https://isr.uci.edu/node/425.html)
- "But you are free" (BYAF) meta-analysis (Fillon, Souchet, Pascual, Girandola; Metapsychology, 2023-07-10): 52 experiments (N=19,528), overall g=0.44 (95% CI 0.36-0.51), stronger face-to-face; but the seven low-risk-of-bias studies showed no effect (g=0.11, 95% CI -0.18 to 0.40); expected discovery rate ~6%. [Peer-reviewed meta-analysis][F] — [Metapsychology](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2640)
- Reactance: chatbot messages perceived as threatening control or crossing boundaries trigger reactance and lower satisfaction (MIS Quarterly 2026). [Peer-reviewed][F] — [USF](https://www.usf.edu/business/news/2026/04-20-chatbot-empathy-can-worsen-customer-reactions-usf-study.aspx)
- HHS HIPAA right-of-access FAQs (a model for "explain risk, confirm, respect choice"): individuals may receive their PHI by unencrypted email if they request it; the covered entity must give a brief warning that a third party could read it in transit and confirm the individual still wants it, then comply; it is not responsible for in-transit disclosure after the warning was accepted, but remains responsible for reasonable safeguards such as correctly entering the email address. [Regulator][S] — [HHS FAQ 2060](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/2060/do-individuals-have-the-right-under-hipaa-to-have/index.html); [HHS FAQ 2061](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/document/faq-2061-covered-entity-responsible-if-it-complies-individuals-access-request-receive-phi)
- CFPB: chatbots risk failing to recognize when consumers invoke legal rights and create privacy/data-security risk (cites the Inbenta/Ticketmaster chatbot-vendor breach: 9.4M data subjects, 60,000 payment card details). [Regulator][F] — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/)
- Stop signals: DSA Art. 25 names re-asking for a choice already made as a manipulative pattern; FCA "sludge"; Fin warns a "flat refusal ... with nowhere to go" is worse than no guidance; Copilot Studio and Google stop after two attempts. [Law/Vendor doc] — [DSA Art. 25](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-25/); [fin.ai](https://fin.ai/blog/blueprint/how-to-set-your-escalation-policy); [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/authoring-system-fallback-topic); [Google](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/errors)
- LLM persuasion is strong, especially when personalized (Salvi et al. 2025), so persuasion limits matter more for an LLM agent than for a scripted bot. [Peer-reviewed][S] — [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v9y2025i8d10.1038_s41562-025-02194-6.html)

### Inferences
- Gate explanation structure: (1) what is needed; (2) one sentence of why, framed as protecting the user's own claim, payout and data; (3) alternatives (a different identifier, a secure link/app, phone); (4) what the agent can still do without verification (general process info); (5) human option.
- Example wording (my synthesis, not sourced): "Before I can open your claim details I need to confirm it's you. That's what stops anyone else from seeing your claim or changing where a payment goes. I can use your claim number plus date of birth, or your policy number plus ZIP code. If you'd rather not do that here, I can still explain how the claims process works, or connect you with someone who can verify you by phone."
- Consent gates (e.g., sharing with a third party, emailing sensitive details) follow the HHS pattern: plain-language risk statement, explicit yes/no, record the answer, respect it.
- Stop rule: explain once; answer one follow-up question about why; after a second refusal or two failed attempts, stop persuading and offer alternatives or a human; never bypass a gate or hint at workarounds; never re-raise a declined consent later in the same conversation (DSA-style anti-nagging).
- Keep explanations to one or two sentences (Baymard: users mainly need the reason; Knijnenburg & Kobsa: heavier justification can lower trust). Autonomy statements ("it's your choice") should be genuine information, not a compliance trick; the BYAF evidence is weak anyway.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found on chatbot explanations for identity verification and their effect on completion; no insurer-published verification scripts found.
- KBA pass-rate statistics circulating in search results (e.g., genuine customers passing KBA ~46% vs fraudsters ~92%, attributed to voice-authentication vendor Pindrop) could not be verified at source.

## 6. Off-topic and scope refusal UX: declining and redirecting without sounding robotic, retry counters, when to offer a human

### Takeaway
Documented practice is a brief, non-judgmental decline plus a one-line scope statement and a redirect to what the agent can do, with varied wording on repeats; after about two failed or off-topic attempts, offer a human or another resource. Guardrails must also stop brand-damaging output (DPD) and invented policy (Cursor, Air Canada), because companies are held to what their bots say.

### Cited Findings
- Claude docs customer-support guide (built on a car-insurance example): off-topic -> "Clarifies it does not discuss unrelated topics and steers the user back to car insurance"; "Stay on topic (car insurance)"; "Redirect off-topic questions back to relevant subjects"; sample guardrails: "If asked about an insurance type we don't offer, politely state that we don't provide that service."; "Do not speculate about future product offerings or company plans."; "Don't make promises or enter into agreements it's not authorized to make. You only provide information and guidance."; "Do not mention any competitor's products or services." [Vendor doc][F] — [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/use-case-guides/customer-support-chat)
- Intercom scope template: "You are a support assistant for [Your Company] and should only help users with questions and tasks directly related to [Your Product] ... Do not complete general-purpose tasks such as rewriting unrelated documents, drafting personal emails, summarizing unrelated content, or acting as a general writing assistant. If a user asks you to perform a task unrelated to [Your Product], politely decline and clarify your scope". [Vendor doc][F] — [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/10560969-fin-guidance-best-practices-and-examples)
- Google conversation design: first a "rapid reprompt" ("Quickly and concisely collect the information again or in another way"; "Do not repeat the original prompt verbatim since it will sound robotic"); second, escalate detail with options/examples; "Assume the user is being cooperative, and what they're saying is relevant and valid"; be transparent about errors; end after 2 No Match attempts. [Vendor doc][F] — [Google](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/errors)
- Copilot Studio: two reprompts then Escalate; the unrecognized input is stored in `UnrecognizedTriggerPhrase` for routing or analysis. [Vendor doc][F] — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/authoring-system-fallback-topic)
- DPD (UK), January 2024: a customer who couldn't get parcel help coaxed the chatbot into swearing and writing a poem disparaging DPD ("There was once a chatbot named DPD, Who was useless at providing help"); DPD disabled the AI element on 2024-01-18; the post was viewed 800,000 times in 24 hours. [Press][S] — [Employee Benefits](https://employeebenefits.co.uk/dpd-disables-ai-online-chatbot-after-it-criticises-service-to-a-customer); [SCMP](https://scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3249284/uk-delivery-firm-dpd-suspends-ai-chat-function-after-bot-swears-customer-and-writes-poem-disparaging)
- Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149 (Feb 2024): the airline was liable for negligent misrepresentation by its website chatbot about bereavement fares; the tribunal rejected the argument that the chatbot was a separate entity; award C$650.88 damages plus interest and fees. [Law][S] — [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/moffatt-v-air-canada-misrepresentation-ai-chatbot)
- CFPB: "doom loops" with no off-ramp to a human damage trust and may breach consumer-protection obligations. [Regulator][F] — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/)

### Inferences
- Decline pattern: (1) one-line decline without moralizing plus scope ("I can only help with your claim and policy here"); (2) redirect with 2-3 concrete things the agent can do; (3) if the user persists, offer a human or the right resource.
- Keep an off-topic counter separate from frustration/verification counters; reset it on an on-topic turn; at 2 consecutive off-topic turns offer a human/other channel; at 3 close politely. Vary the decline wording each time (Google).
- Assume cooperation first: a confused claimant may phrase a claim question in an unexpected way; ask one clarifying question before classifying as off-topic.
- Treat "adjacent but out of scope" requests (legal advice on suing the other driver, medical advice, guarantees of coverage or payout amounts, repair-shop recommendations) as reasons to route to a licensed adjuster/human, not as flat refusals.
- Hard guardrails: answer only from approved knowledge; "I don't know, let me get a person" is an acceptable answer; no content about the company, competitors or the bot itself beyond scope (DPD).

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence on the optimal off-topic retry count; vendor defaults (two) are the only reference found.
- No user research found comparing refusal phrasings for LLM support agents.

## 7. Post-interaction follow-up: summary emails, consent, CAN-SPAM/TCPA, address confirmation, vendor implementations and failures

### Takeaway
Customer-facing AI-generated summary emails are not a documented mainstream feature of Intercom, Zendesk, Ada, Decagon or Sierra: vendors offer transcripts, and AI summaries are mostly agent-facing. A requested claim-conversation summary should qualify as a CAN-SPAM transactional/relationship message if it carries no promotional content; TCPA matters only if sent by SMS; HIPAA applies to health claims. Analogous failures (Apple's false summaries, Otter's over-sharing, Air Canada's liability) point to: explicit opt-in, verified address on file, minimal sensitive data, status taken from the system of record, LLM limited to grounded narrative, and user confirmation before sending.

### Cited Findings
**Vendor implementations**
- Zendesk: in legacy Live Chat, agents can "Export transcript" and email it, and transcripts go to the email the end user entered in the web widget; in Messaging, end users can't request a transcript but see the whole conversation in the widget; if admins set transcript visibility to public, the transcript is added to the ticket and emailed when the ticket is updated or closed. [Vendor doc/community][S] — [Zendesk](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408833687450)
- Intercom: no built-in automatic transcript email at close; users/leads can download a .txt transcript in the Messenger (desktop only); Intercom emails a user/lead ~2 minutes after an unseen teammate reply if an email is on file; automated transcripts require a custom API build. [Vendor doc/community][S] — [Intercom community](https://community.intercom.com/messenger-8/do-users-always-receive-an-email-transcript-of-the-intercom-conversation-1124); [Intercom FAQ](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/8838326-conversations-faqs)
- Dialpad "AI Recap": post-call summary with action items delivered to the Dialpad inbox (internal, not customer-facing). [Vendor doc][S] — [Dialpad](https://www.dialpad.com/blog/ai-recaps-powered-by-dialpadgpt/)

**Law**
- CAN-SPAM: a message is transactional/relationship if it consists only of content that (1) "Facilitates, completes, or confirms a commercial transaction that the recipient already has agreed to"; (2) "gives warranty, recall, safety, or security information about a product or service the recipient bought"; (3) "notifies the recipient about a change in the terms or features of a membership, subscription, account, loan or other ongoing commercial relationship"; (4) employment/benefits information; (5) "delivers goods or services as part of a transaction that the recipient already has agreed to". Mixed messages are commercial if a recipient reading the subject line would think it is an ad, or if the transactional content "does not appear mainly at the beginning of the message". Transactional messages still may not contain false or misleading routing information. The categories are read narrowly. Penalties up to $53,088 per violating email. [Regulator][F] — [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
- TCPA covers calls/texts, not email. FCC revocation-of-consent rules effective 2025-04-11: consumers may revoke "through any reasonable manner"; words like stop, quit, end, revoke conclusively revoke; honor within 10 business days; one confirmation text allowed within five minutes with no marketing content. The FCC later postponed compliance with part of the rule by one year. [Law-firm summaries][S] — [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/fcc-sets-effective-date-for-tcpa-reasonable-consent-revocation-rules); [Brownstein](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/fcc-announces-effective-date-for-new-revocation-of-consent-order/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/telecoms-mobile-cable-communications/1610452/fcc-extends-deadline-for-tcpa-consent-revocation-rule-compliance-by-one-year)
- HIPAA (health claims): unencrypted email is allowed when the individual requests it after a risk warning and confirmation; the sender stays responsible for reasonable safeguards such as entering the address correctly. [Regulator][S] — [HHS FAQ 2060](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/2060/do-individuals-have-the-right-under-hipaa-to-have/index.html); [HHS FAQ 2061](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/document/faq-2061-covered-entity-responsible-if-it-complies-individuals-access-request-receive-phi)

**Customer value of follow-up**
- J.D. Power 2025 U.S. Claims Digital Experience Study (released Dec 2025; 5,958 auto/home claimants): receiving adequate digital updates is a top driver of satisfaction, but insurers deliver on it only 22% of the time; satisfaction is highest with app-delivered status updates, which only 36% of auto and 31% of home claimants receive. [Survey][S] — [J.D. Power](https://www.jdpower.com/business/press-releases/2025-us-claims-digital-experience-study/)
- 83% of consumers say they repeat themselves after transfers (Five9 2026), a further argument for a written record of what was said. [Survey][F] — [CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/customer-experience/customers-the-ai-handoff-is-broken-contact-center-leaders-wont-hear-it/)

**What has gone wrong (analogous incidents)**
- Apple Intelligence (Dec 2024 - Jan 2025): notification summaries produced false headlines (a BBC story summarized as claiming Luigi Mangione had taken his own life; NYT stories merged into a false claim that Netanyahu had been arrested); Apple made news summaries "temporarily unavailable" in iOS 18.3 and said summaries would more clearly signal AI generation; BBC: the summaries "do not reflect — and in some cases completely contradict — the original BBC content". [Press][S] — [ABC7/CNN, 2025-01-16](https://abc7news.com/post/apple-is-pulling-ai-generated-notifications-news-generating-fake-headlines/15810860/)
- Otter.ai (Sept 2024): an automatically emailed meeting transcript included hours of private discussion recorded after the call ended and went to every invitee; the deal fell through; litigation followed. [Press][S] — [UC Today](https://www.uctoday.com/security-compliance-risk/otter-ai-lawsuit-meeting-bot-recording-after-call/)
- Air Canada (2024): companies are liable for what their chatbot tells customers (see Q6). [Law][S] — [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/moffatt-v-air-canada-misrepresentation-ai-chatbot)
- CFPB: chatbots "often generate incorrect outputs that are undetectable by some users". [Regulator][F] — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/chatbots-in-consumer-finance/)

### Inferences
- Consent: offer once, at the end, only after a substantive conversation; opt-in, default "no"; e.g. "Would you like a summary of today's chat and your next steps emailed to the address on your policy (j•••@g•••.com)?" Record yes/no, timestamp and destination; if declined, don't ask again.
- Address: send only to the verified address on file. If the user types a different address, don't send claim specifics there (misdelivery, typo and account-takeover risk); offer a generic "your summary is ready in the secure portal" notice or an address-change process with verification. (HHS's "correctly entering the email address" duty supports care; the account-takeover reasoning is mine.)
- Content: (1) what we discussed (bullets); (2) claim status as of a timestamp, copied from the system of record, not paraphrased by the LLM; (3) actions completed in the chat (e.g., photos received); (4) next steps with owner and deadline; (5) masked claim/policy reference; (6) how to reply or correct errors; (7) a note that it is an AI-generated summary and that official decisions come in formal notices.
- Exclude: verification answers, DOB, SSN, full policy/claim numbers, bank details, medical details, internal notes or sentiment labels, speculation on coverage/payout, anything said after the user ended the session (Otter), and any marketing (keeps CAN-SPAM transactional status and avoids the mixed-content primary-purpose test).
- Accuracy: build the summary from the structured event log (tool calls, statuses, documents); let the LLM only phrase the narrative; run a faithfulness check against the transcript; show the draft in chat for the user to confirm before sending.
- If SMS is offered instead of email, TCPA consent and STOP-style revocation handling apply.

### Gaps
- No documentation found of a customer-facing "send me a summary" AI feature in Ada, Decagon, Sierra or Intercom Fin, and no public postmortem of an AI-generated customer-service summary email that leaked PII or misstated an outcome; the incidents above are analogies.
- GLBA, state insurance privacy and data-security rules (e.g., NAIC model laws) and GDPR lawful basis for summary emails were not researched.
