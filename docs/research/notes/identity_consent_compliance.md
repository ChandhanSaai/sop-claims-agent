# Identity Verification, Third-Party Consent, PII Handling and Regulatory Constraints for Insurance/Healthcare Claims Chat Agents

Research notes compiled 2026-10-07. Every finding is tagged so the report writer can separate binding law from practice:
[LAW] statute or regulation text; [LAW-MODEL] NAIC model law (binding only where a state adopted it); [REG-GUIDANCE] regulator FAQ, bulletin or letter; [STANDARD] NIST/OWASP-type standard (binding only on certain entities); [PRACTICE] observed government or industry operating practice; [VENDOR] vendor documentation or vendor-reported data; [SECONDARY] law-firm, news or blog summary not verified against the primary text. Target design referenced throughout: a claims chat agent that verifies 3 of 5 identifiers (full name, DOB, phone, email, SSN/national-ID last 4), supports a policyholder's son as an authorized representative only with the policyholder's consent, and offers an end-of-conversation email summary.

## 1. KBA practice in insurance, healthcare and banking contact centers: data points, matching, attempt limits, generic failures, no echo, step-up, escalation

### Takeaway
No US law sets a number of knowledge-based data points; HIPAA, GLBA and state insurance law require "verification" or "reasonable safeguards" and leave the method to the entity. The best-documented public protocols (IRS, Medicare contractors, 1-800-MEDICARE) require a fixed set of elements that must all match (not "any N of M"), never confirm or deny account information before authentication completes, add probes for higher-risk accounts, and require written or recorded authority for third parties. Enumeration-safe generic failure messages, per-account attempt counters and step-up to a possession factor come from security standards, not from statute.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] HIPAA 45 CFR 164.514(h)(1): "Prior to any disclosure permitted by this subpart, a covered entity must: (i) Except with respect to disclosures under § 164.510, verify the identity of a person requesting protected health information and the authority of any such person to have access to protected health information under this subpart, if the identity or any such authority of such person is not known to the covered entity; and (ii) Obtain any documentation, statements, or representations, whether oral or written, from the person requesting the protected health information when such documentation, statement, or representation is a condition of the disclosure." Paragraph (h)(2) covers reliance on documentation, public officials, and professional judgment. No method or number of identifiers is specified. — [45 CFR 164.514 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.514)
- [PRACTICE] IRS phone/visitor authentication (IRM 21.1.3, revision dated Oct 1, 2026): "Do not confirm or deny any taxpayer information until authentication has been successfully completed" (IRM 21.1.3.2.3(4)); assistors must complete the required IMF/BMF authentication probes (cross-referenced to IRM 10.10.3.3.6) before disclosure; additional authentication is required for higher-risk situations, e.g. accounts with identity-theft markers, requests to mail transcripts to an address other than the address of record, IP PIN issues (IRM 21.1.3.2.3(8)); failed authentication is documented in the case system. — [IRS IRM 21.1.3](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part21/irm_21-001-003). Caveat: the fetch tool's summary also said assistors do not reveal which question failed; that wording was not verified verbatim.
- [PRACTICE] Medicare Administrative Contractor (CGS, J15) provider contact center, effective March 1, 2024: callers "must authenticate" all of: NPI, PTAN, last five digits of the TIN, Medicare Beneficiary Identifier, "Beneficiary's first initial", "Beneficiary's last name – Enter up to six letters ... (ignore any spaces)", and "The beneficiary's date of birth." This is an all-elements exact-match protocol with deterministic name normalization (first initial + first six letters of surname, spaces ignored). — [CGS Medicare, Feb 1, 2024 (reviewed Aug 12, 2026)](https://www.cgsmedicare.com/jc/pubs/news/2024/02/cope150389.html)
- [PRACTICE] 1-800-MEDICARE: "By law, you must give 1-800-MEDICARE permission in writing before 1-800-MEDICARE can share any information with someone other than you." The authorization form captures the beneficiary's name, Medicare number, DOB (mm/dd/yyyy), mailing address, phone and email. — [CMS-10106, form version 03/2026](https://www.cms.gov/cms10106-authorization-disclose-personal-health-information)
- [STANDARD] OWASP Authentication Cheat Sheet: do not return reason-specific failures such as "Login for User foo: invalid password" or "This email address doesn't exist in our database"; use one generic message ("Login failed; Invalid user ID or password") so attackers cannot enumerate accounts. For lockout, configure a threshold, an observation window and a lockout duration; count failures per account, not per IP; keep a recovery path available so lockout cannot be weaponized as denial of service. — [OWASP Authentication Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html)
- [STANDARD] NIST SP 800-63B-4 (final, page dated Aug 26, 2025): "The verifier SHALL limit consecutive failed authentication attempts using a specific authenticator on a single subscriber account to no more than 100 by disabling that authenticator"; lower limits may be set by risk assessment. (This applies to authenticators such as passwords and OTPs in federal systems, not to KBA, which NIST does not accept; see section 2.) — [NIST SP 800-63B-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)
- [STANDARD] Step-up by one-time code: NIST SP 800-63B-4 says "Use of the PSTN for out-of-band verification is restricted"; verifiers "SHALL ensure that alternative authenticator types are available to all subscribers", "SHOULD remind subscribers of this limitation of PSTN out-of-band authenticators", and "SHOULD consider risk indicators (e.g., device swap, SIM change, number porting, abnormal behavior)". — [NIST SP 800-63B-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)
- [REG-GUIDANCE] NYDFS (Oct 16, 2024) to all DFS-regulated entities, which include NY-licensed insurers: "Covered Entities should consider using authentication factors that can withstand AI-manipulated deepfakes and other AI-enhanced attacks by avoiding authentication via SMS text, voice, or video, and using forms of authentication that AI deepfakes cannot impersonate, such as digital-based certificates and physical security keys." — [NYDFS Industry Letter, Oct 16, 2024](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/industry-letters/il20241016-cyber-risks-ai-and-strategies-combat-related-risks)
- [VENDOR] Twilio Verify (a common OTP step-up service) supports "SMS, Passkeys, Silent Network Auth, Automatic Channel Selection, Voice, WhatsApp, Email, TOTP ..., Push and Silent Device Approval"; a verification's status can be "pending, approved, canceled, max_attempts_reached, deleted, failed or expired", i.e. the service itself enforces attempt caps and expiry; SMS verifications support a per-attempt risk check tied to Fraud Guard and geo permissions. — [Twilio Verify API overview](https://www.twilio.com/docs/verify/api); [Twilio Verification resource](https://www.twilio.com/docs/verify/api/verification)
- [VENDOR] Why a bot must never echo identifiers: Amazon Lex V2 slot obfuscation replaces slot values with the slot name in conversation logs (e.g. "My name is {full_name}"), but "If you are using slot values in your prompts or responses, those slot values are not obfuscated in conversation logs"; values stored in request/session attributes are not obfuscated; audio is not obfuscated (only its transcription); and "Any slot values in missed utterances won't be obfuscated." — [AWS Lex V2 docs: obscuring slot values](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lexv2/latest/dg/monitoring-obfuscate.html)
- [VENDOR] Pindrop case study: 12% of the time, agents at retail and insurance companies failed to verify callers' identities (2022 Voice Intelligence & Security Report). — [BusinessWire, Apr 20, 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220420005041/en)

### Inferences
- "Any 3 of 5" is not a recognized regulatory or published-protocol standard. Public protocols (IRS, CMS/MAC) use a fixed required set. Under an any-combination rule a caller holding only name + phone + email (low-secrecy data that is often public or in breach dumps) passes without DOB or SSN-4. Safer variants: treat the name as the lookup key or a prerequisite rather than a factor, then require at least 2 of the remaining 4 with at least one of DOB or SSN-4; or treat phone and email as possession factors by sending a code to the on-file value instead of asking the caller to recite them.
- Matching rules (no authoritative source found; design inference): normalize before comparing. Names: case-fold, strip diacritics, punctuation and repeated spaces; accept variants (hyphenated, maiden/married, nicknames) only if they are stored as aliases on the record, never by LLM judgment. DOB: parse to an ISO date; if input is ambiguous (03/04/1980), ask for the month by name or use a date picker rather than guessing. Phone: normalize to E.164 (strip +1, spaces, dashes, parentheses). Email: trim and lowercase. SSN-4: exactly four digits. A near-miss counts as a miss. The CGS "first initial + first six letters, spaces ignored" rule shows a deterministic, documentable normalization.
- Generic failure: respond identically whether the claim/policy does not exist, the lookup key is wrong, or one field mismatched ("I wasn't able to verify your identity with the details provided"). Never say which field failed and never give partial credit ("2 of 3 matched"). This follows from the IRS "do not confirm or deny" rule, OWASP enumeration guidance, and the fact that the existence of a customer relationship is itself protected (section 4).
- Attempt limits: count failures per claim/policy record across sessions, not per chat session, so a new chat cannot reset the counter. After a small number of failures, stop KBA and offer a one-time code to an on-file contact or a human handoff instead of a hard lock (OWASP: keep a recovery path so lockout cannot be weaponized). Log every failure with the session ID.
- Never echo submitted identifiers ("I have your DOB as March 4, 1980"). Confirm with "Thanks, you're verified" only. Echoing also defeats log redaction, per the Lex caveat.
- Escalation to a human must apply the same verification rules; the human agent must not be able to override a failed verification without a stronger factor (see the 2025 help-desk social engineering in section 2).

### Gaps
- No authoritative industry survey (ICMI, CCW, Gartner, Forrester) was found that states typical KBA data-point counts, attempt limits (the common "3 tries" figure) or lockout durations for insurance or health-plan contact centers; treat any such number as unsourced.
- The HHS individual-access guidance page (commonly cited for the point that HIPAA mandates no particular verification method and verification must not unreasonably delay access) returned HTTP 403 to every fetch method and could not be quoted.
- Twilio Verify's default code lifetime and maximum check attempts could not be confirmed from the fetched pages.
- No source was found on handling DOB-format ambiguity or name aliases in contact centers specifically; the guidance above is inference.

## 2. Known weaknesses of KBA (NIST, social engineering, breach exposure) and compensating controls

### Takeaway
NIST's final Digital Identity Guidelines (SP 800-63-4, August 2025) prohibit knowledge-based verification for identity verification and prohibit KBA or security questions as authenticators, allowing KBV only as a fraud-management signal. Vendor data shows fraudsters pass KBA more reliably than genuine customers, SSNs are partly predictable from public data, and a June 2025 social-engineering campaign hit several US insurers through help desks. Compensating controls in use or recommended: possession-based step-up to on-file channels, telecom risk signals (SIM swap, porting), deepfake-resistant factors, data minimization, and limiting what KBA alone unlocks.

### Cited Findings
- [STANDARD] NIST SP 800-63A-4 §2.5.1: "Knowledge-based verification (KBV) or knowledge-based authentication SHALL NOT be used for identity verification." §3.2 (fraud management): "CSPs MAY employ KBV as part of its fraud management program." Page dated Aug 26, 2025. — [NIST SP 800-63A-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63a.html)
- [STANDARD] NIST SP 800-63B-4: "Verifiers and CSPs SHALL NOT prompt subscribers to use knowledge-based authentication (KBA) (e.g., 'What was the name of your first pet?') or security questions when choosing passwords." Account recovery (Section 4.2) relies on recovery codes and other methods, not security questions. — [NIST SP 800-63B-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)
- [VENDOR] Pindrop 2022 Voice Intelligence & Security Report: in a call-center case study, fraudsters passed KBA questions 92% of the time while genuine customers passed only 46% of the time; data dealers sell organized stolen personal data. — [BusinessWire, Apr 20, 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220420005041/en); [BetaNews, Apr 20, 2022](https://betanews.com/2022/04/20/fraudsters-better-than-customers-at-answering-security-questions/)
- [ACADEMIC] Acquisti & Gross, PNAS 2009: SSNs can be statistically predicted from date and place of birth using public data; they identified the first five digits for 44% of deceased individuals born after 1988 and all nine digits for 8.5% of them in fewer than 1,000 attempts. — [Acquisti & Gross, PNAS 2009 (PDF)](https://www.heinz.cmu.edu/~acquisti/papers/AcquistiGross-PNAS-2009.pdf)
- [NEWS] June 2025 Scattered Spider campaign against US insurers: Erie Insurance and Philadelphia Insurance Companies reported network incidents on June 7 and June 9, 2025; Aflac identified unauthorized access on June 12, 2025 and attributed it to social engineering; the group calls help desks posing as employees to get credentials reset or MFA bypassed. — [The Register, Jun 20, 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/06/20/aflac_scattered_spider/); [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/3-key-takeaways-from-the-scattered-spider-attacks-on-insurance-firms/amp/)
- [SECONDARY] A Dec 30, 2025 breach-analysis site reported Aflac's final impact as about 22.65 million customers, beneficiaries, employees and agents, with exposed data including SSNs, government IDs, medical and health-insurance information and claims data. Not verified against Aflac's own filings. — [breached.company, Dec 30, 2025](https://breached.company/aflac-data-breach-exposes-22-65-million-in-scattered-spider-insurance-campaign/)
- [REG-GUIDANCE] NYDFS (Oct 16, 2024): threat actors use AI deepfakes via email, phone ("vishing") and text; "deepfakes have been used to mimic an individual's appearance or voice in an attempt to authenticate that individual and circumvent biometric verification technology"; recommends deepfake-resistant factors, liveness detection or multi-modal biometrics; and states: "As of November 2025, the Cybersecurity Regulation will require MFA to be in place for all Authorized Users attempting to access Covered Entities' Information Systems or NPI, including customers, employees, contractors, and TPSPs." — [NYDFS Industry Letter, Oct 16, 2024](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/industry-letters/il20241016-cyber-risks-ai-and-strategies-combat-related-risks)
- [LAW] GLBA anti-pretexting, 15 U.S.C. 6821(a): unlawful to obtain or attempt to obtain customer information of a financial institution by "making a false, fictitious, or fraudulent statement or representation to an officer, employee, or agent of a financial institution", by false statements to a customer, or by providing a document known to be "forged, counterfeit, lost, or stolen"; (b) bars soliciting others to do so. — [15 U.S.C. 6821 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/6821)

### Inferences
- All five fixture identifiers are KBA-class data. Name, DOB, phone and email are routinely available from breaches and data brokers; SSN-4 is the part of the SSN that is not predictable from birth data (per Acquisti & Gross) but it is exposed in breaches such as Aflac's. A 3-of-5 KBA gate is therefore low assurance: defensible for read-only, low-sensitivity answers (claim status, next steps), not for high-risk actions (changing payee or bank details, contact info, mailing address, sending documents to a new destination). Require a one-time code to an on-file channel for those.
- For NYDFS-regulated insurers, the November 2025 MFA requirement "including customers" may make KBA-only access to NPI through a chat channel hard to defend. Whether a chatbot conversation counts as an "Authorized User" accessing "Information Systems or NPI" is an open question for counsel.
- NIST treats SMS/voice codes as restricted and NYDFS recommends avoiding SMS/voice for deepfake resistance, so the preferred step-up is an email code or app push to on-file contacts, with SMS as a fallback gated on SIM-swap/porting signals.
- Help-desk escalation must not become the bypass: humans follow the same verification and cannot override a failure.
- Whether an LLM chatbot is an "agent of a financial institution" under 15 U.S.C. 6821 is untested (no source found), but lying to the bot to obtain someone else's claim data is the conduct the statute targets.

### Gaps
- No 2023-2026 primary data found on KBA pass rates in chat (as opposed to voice) channels; the Pindrop 92%/46% figure is a 2022 vendor-reported single case study.
- Pindrop 2024/2025 deepfake statistics and Nuance Gatekeeper documentation were not retrieved.
- NIST SP 800-63-4 binds federal agencies; no source was found of a state insurance regulator formally adopting it for consumer authentication.

## 3. HIPAA requirements relevant to a claims chatbot

### Takeaway
For health-plan claims, HIPAA requires: verifying identity and authority of any requester not already known (164.514(h)); minimum-necessary limits except for disclosures to the individual (164.502(b)); treating legally authorized personal representatives as the individual (164.502(g)); and, separately, it permits disclosures of directly relevant PHI to family or others involved in payment with the individual's agreement or non-objection, or by professional judgment when the individual is absent (164.510(b)). An LLM API that processes PHI is a business associate needing a BAA, and only BAA-covered features may be used. The Security Rule requires audit controls and person-or-entity authentication; required documentation is retained six years. The proposed Security Rule update that would mandate MFA was still not final as of mid-2026.

### Cited Findings
- [LAW] Verification: 45 CFR 164.514(h)(1) as quoted in section 1; it expressly excepts 164.510 disclosures, which rely on professional judgment ((h)(2) covers professional judgment). — [45 CFR 164.514 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.514)
- [LAW] Minimum necessary, 45 CFR 164.502(b)(1): a covered entity "must make reasonable efforts to limit protected health information to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose of the use, disclosure, or request." Exceptions (b)(2) include "(ii) Uses or disclosures made to the individual" and "(iii) Uses or disclosures made pursuant to an authorization under § 164.508". 164.514(d)(3)(i): for routine and recurring disclosures, implement "standard protocols" limiting PHI to the amount reasonably necessary. — [45 CFR 164.502 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.502); [45 CFR 164.514 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.514)
- [LAW] Personal representatives, 45 CFR 164.502(g)(1)-(2): a covered entity "must ... treat a personal representative as the individual"; "If under applicable law a person has authority to act on behalf of an individual who is an adult or an emancipated minor in making decisions related to health care, a covered entity must treat such person as a personal representative." (g)(5) lets the entity decline where it reasonably believes the individual "has been or may be subjected to domestic violence, abuse, or neglect by such person" or that treating the person as representative "could endanger the individual." — [45 CFR 164.502 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.502)
- [LAW] Family and others involved in payment, 45 CFR 164.510(b)(1)(i): a covered entity may disclose "to a family member, other relative, or a close personal friend of the individual, or any other person identified by the individual, the protected health information directly relevant to such person's involvement with the individual's health care or payment related to the individual's health care." (b)(2), individual present and capable: permitted if the entity "(i) Obtains the individual's agreement; (ii) Provides the individual with the opportunity to object to the disclosure, and the individual does not express an objection; or (iii) Reasonably infers from the circumstances, based on the exercise of professional judgment, that the individual does not object." (b)(3), individual not present or incapacitated: the entity may, in professional judgment, disclose "only the protected health information that is directly relevant to the person's involvement" if in the individual's best interests. — [45 CFR 164.510 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.510)
- [REG-GUIDANCE] HHS FAQ 1067 "May a health plan disclose protected health information to a person who calls the plan on the beneficiary's behalf?" Answer: yes, subject to 164.510(b). Examples: "A health plan may disclose relevant PHI to a beneficiary's daughter who has called to assist her hospitalized, elderly mother in resolving a claims or other payment issue"; to "a human resources representative who has called the plan with the beneficiary also on the line, or who could turn the phone over to the beneficiary, who could then confirm for the plan that the representative calling is assisting the beneficiary"; to a Congressional office that faxed the beneficiary's letter requesting help. Non-family persons require "reasonable assurance that the person has been identified by the individual as being involved in his or her care or payment." Created 3/14/2006, content last reviewed 7/26/2013, still published (page metadata modified Sept 2026). — [HHS FAQ 1067](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/1067/may-a-health-plan-disclose-information-to-a-person-who-calls/index.html)
- [REG-GUIDANCE] What counts as PHI before verification: "Identifying information alone, such as personal names, residential addresses, or phone numbers, would not necessarily be designated as PHI... If such information was listed with health condition, health care provision or payment data, such as an indication that the individual was treated at a certain clinic, then this information would be PHI." — [HHS de-identification guidance (page updated Mar 2026)](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/de-identification/index.html)
- [LAW] Accounting of disclosures, 45 CFR 164.528(a): individuals may obtain an accounting of disclosures "in the six years prior to the date on which the accounting is requested", excluding disclosures for treatment, payment and operations, to the individual, pursuant to an authorization, to persons involved in care under 164.510, incidental disclosures, limited data sets, and others. — [45 CFR 164.528 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.528)
- [LAW] Security Rule technical safeguards, 45 CFR 164.312: (b) audit controls: "Implement hardware, software, and/or procedural mechanisms that record and examine activity in information systems that contain or use electronic protected health information"; (d) person or entity authentication: "Implement procedures to verify that a person or entity seeking access to electronic protected health information is the one claimed"; (e)(2)(ii) encryption in transmission "whenever deemed appropriate" (addressable). — [45 CFR 164.312 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.312)
- [LAW] Documentation and retention, 45 CFR 164.530(j): maintain required written communications and records of required actions "in written or electronic form" and retain them "for six years from the date of its creation or the date when it last was in effect, whichever is later"; 164.530(c): appropriate administrative, technical and physical safeguards, and "reasonably safeguard protected health information to limit incidental uses or disclosures". — [45 CFR 164.530 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.530)
- [REG-GUIDANCE] Business associates and cloud/LLM vendors: "When a covered entity engages the services of a CSP to create, receive, maintain, or transmit ePHI ... the CSP is a business associate under HIPAA ... This is true even if the CSP processes or stores only encrypted ePHI and lacks an encryption key for the data. As a result, the covered entity (or business associate) and the CSP must enter into a HIPAA-compliant business associate agreement (BAA)." The conduit exception "is limited to transmission-only services"; a CSP that becomes aware it holds ePHI must comply or "securely return the ePHI ... or ... securely destroy" it. — [HHS Guidance on HIPAA & Cloud Computing (2016, page updated Mar 2026)](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html)
- [VENDOR] Anthropic (privacy center, updated October 2026): provides a BAA for "HIPAA-ready services, such as use of our first-party API, Claude in Microsoft Foundry, or Enterprise plans"; to use the first-party API with PHI the organization signs the BAA and has it enabled. Covered Messages API features: prompt caching, structured outputs, memory, web search, bash tool, text editor tool (under BAA versions accepted after 4/1/26); Batch API, Files API, Skills API, Code Execution, Computer Use and Web Fetch are "Not covered under Anthropic BAA and not accessible for HIPAA-Ready API users"; data sent to third parties via External MCP is not covered; "Covered Models require 30-day data retention and aren't available with zero data retention (ZDR) enabled unless Anthropic has expressly authorized it." — [Anthropic: BAAs for commercial customers](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/8114513-business-associate-agreements-baa-for-commercial-customers)
- [VENDOR] OpenAI (help center, updated early Oct 2026): eligible organizations accept the standard BAA in the API Platform (Settings > Organization > General > HIPAA compliance support > Enable); "Self-serve enrollment requires an established history of API usage"; custom BAAs via baa@openai.com; eligible services are listed on a separate "HIPAA eligible products and functionality" page. — [OpenAI: Getting a BAA for the OpenAI API](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8660679-getting-a-business-associate-agreement-for-the-openai-api). [SECONDARY] Third-party guides state PHI may only go to zero-data-retention-eligible endpoints and that standard endpoints retain data 30 days. — [Arkenea](https://arkenea.com/blog/is-openai-hipaa-compliant-2025-guide/)
- [SECONDARY] HIPAA Security Rule update: OCR's December 2024 NPRM would make MFA a required (not addressable) specification for access to ePHI systems; OCR's regulatory agenda targeted May 2026 for a final rule, but as of June 2026 it remained a proposal with no confirmed date; OCR continues to enforce the existing rule. — [ComplianceHub](https://compliancehub.wiki/blog/hipaa-security-rule-final-rule-may-2026-mfa-encryption-uncertainty); [Medcurity](https://medcurity.com/hipaa-security-rule-2026/)
- [REG-GUIDANCE] Unencrypted email to the individual: "individuals have a right to receive a copy of their PHI by unencrypted e-mail if the individual requests access in this manner. In such cases, the covered entity must provide a brief warning to the individual that there is some level of risk that the individual's PHI could be read or otherwise accessed by a third party while in transit, and confirm that the individual still wants to receive her PHI by unencrypted e-mail." A covered entity is not responsible for interception in transit when fulfilling an access request in an unsecure manner the individual was warned of and accepted, but is otherwise responsible for breach notification for unsecured transmissions (FAQ last reviewed May 30, 2025; partly limited by Ciox Health v. Azar). — [HHS FAQ: right to unsecure transmission](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/do-individuals-have-the-right-under-hipaa-to-have/index.html); [HHS FAQ: responsibility for interception](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/is-a-covered-entity-responsible-if-it-complies/index.html)

### Inferences
- Before verification, a health-plan bot should not confirm that a person is a member or has a claim, because name plus an indication of payment data is PHI. Pre-verification replies should be limited to general process information (how claims work, documents needed, timelines, how to verify) and be identical whether or not a record exists.
- A bot cannot exercise the "professional judgment" 164.510(b)(3) contemplates, so the target design (son gets access only with the policyholder's explicit consent) is a conservative implementation of 164.510(b)(2)(i). The son is a 164.510 "family member involved in payment" (scope limited to information directly relevant to his involvement) unless he holds a health-care power of attorney or guardianship, in which case he is a 164.502(g) personal representative treated as the individual. These are two different disclosure scopes and should be two different code paths.
- Minimum necessary does not apply to disclosures to the verified policyholder but does apply to the son; even for the policyholder, answering only what was asked is good privacy-by-design practice (not a legal requirement).
- For a health plan or its business associate, every vendor in the PHI path (LLM API, observability/tracing, transcript storage, email delivery) needs a BAA, and only BAA-covered features can be used (e.g. Anthropic's Batch and Files APIs are excluded). P&C and life insurers that are not covered entities do not need BAAs, but GLBA/state vendor-oversight duties apply (section 4).
- Although 164.510 disclosures to family are excluded from the 164.528 accounting, an internal disclosure log (who, what, when, consent basis) supports 164.312(b) audit controls and any 164.530(j) documentation duty.

### Gaps
- The HHS individual-access guidance page (verification method left to the covered entity's discretion) returned HTTP 403 and could not be quoted.
- The Federal Register citation and dates of the Security Rule NPRM were not verified against the primary document; status after June 2026 is unknown.
- No HHS guidance was found that addresses chatbots or LLMs and verification specifically.
- Whether chatbot transcripts become part of a health plan's designated record set (45 CFR 164.501), and so subject to the right of access, was not researched.

## 4. Insurance-specific rules (GLBA, NAIC AI Model Bulletin, Colorado, chatbot disclosure laws, EU AI Act) and which apply to a claims-support bot

### Takeaway
For insurers, GLBA privacy and safeguards are enforced through state insurance law (NAIC Model 672-based privacy regulations, state data-security laws, NYDFS Part 500 in New York). Model 672 treats the bare fact that someone is a customer as protected financial information and requires an opt-in authorization (written or electronic, at most 24 months, revocable) before disclosing nonpublic personal health information outside listed insurance functions. The NAIC AI Model Bulletin, adopted by 25 states plus DC as of Aug 31, 2026, expects a written AI governance program that covers claims, vendor due diligence, and notice to consumers that AI is in use. US chatbot-identity disclosure laws are narrow (California: deception to drive a sale or vote; Maine: when a reasonable consumer could be misled; Utah: on clear request, or up front for high-risk interactions by regulated occupations), and Colorado dropped its blanket AI-interaction disclosure in SB 26-189. EU AI Act Art. 50(1) has required AI-interaction disclosure since Aug 2, 2026.

- Applicability to a claims-support bot (inference; confirm with counsel):

  - NAIC bulletin (in adopting states): put the bot in the AI inventory and governance program, risk-tier it (an informational status bot is lower risk than one that decides or triages claims), do vendor due diligence on the LLM provider, keep audit rights, and give consumers notice that AI is in use (§1.9).

The low-cost design that satisfies all of them plus NAIC §1.9: disclose "I'm an automated assistant" in the first message, answer truthfully whenever asked, and offer a human.

## 5. Authorized representative and third-party flows; consent capture methods, timeouts and fallbacks

### Takeaway
Three legal bases recur. (1) A legal personal representative (power of attorney, guardian) is treated as the individual. (2) HIPAA 164.510(b) permits disclosures to family or others involved in payment with the individual's agreement or non-objection, which HHS illustrates with a daughter resolving her mother's claim and an HR representative with the beneficiary on the line. (3) A standing written or electronic authorization on file (CMS-10106; NAIC Model 672 §19: at most 24 months, revocable). Government practice adds session-scoped oral consent given while the account holder is present (IRS: ends when the call ends) and portal-based proxy access (Kaiser).

### Inferences
Design for the policyholder's-son flow (inference, not a regulatory prescription):
- Path A, authorization on file: look up an unexpired, unrevoked authorization that names the son and its scope (any vs limited: claim status, payments). Verify the son as himself (his name plus a one-time code to his contact on the authorization), never with the policyholder's identifiers, then disclose only within scope. If he presents as a legal personal representative (POA/guardian), route to document verification by staff; the bot should not accept a claimed POA.
- Path B, policyholder present in the same chat: verify the policyholder, then get an explicit yes to a specific prompt naming the person, the information categories, and the duration ("this conversation only"), echoing the IRS ODC model. Weakness: chat cannot prove two people are present; one person can type both roles. Stronger variant: send a consent request to the policyholder's on-file email or phone (link or code) and proceed only on confirmation. No regulator guidance was found that specifically blesses SMS/email consent links; a standing consent captured this way should include the Model 672 §19 elements.
- Path C, no consent: give only general process information; offer to send the policyholder a consent request; never confirm to the son that a claim exists.
- Timeouts: if out-of-band consent is not completed within a short session window (a design choice; no legal source found), close with general information and instructions; do not hold a pending disclosure open indefinitely or across sessions.
- Scope for the son: directly relevant information (status, next steps, outstanding documents, payment status) and never the policyholder's DOB, SSN digits or medical detail beyond what his role requires. Sensitive categories (substance use, mental health, HIV) may need separate consent under state law, as the CMS-10106 New York carve-out shows.
- Record every consent (grantor, grantee, scope, method, timestamp, expiry, revocation) and keep it with the claim record (Model 672 §19D; HIPAA 164.530(j) where documentation is required).

## 6. PII hygiene in LLM systems: redaction, pseudonymization, retention, SSN handling, audit trails, and email-summary minimization

### Takeaway
Redaction is defense-in-depth, not a guarantee: detection tools say they can miss PII, platform log redaction has documented gaps (echoed values, session attributes, audio, missed utterances), and it is off unless configured. Keep verification and authorization in deterministic code (OWASP: never delegate authorization to the LLM), send the model only what it needs (never a full SSN), and minimize and time-limit retention (NYDFS), while reconciling with claim-file rules that require reconstructable records for several years. For email summaries: HHS allows unencrypted email to an individual who asks after a warning; California restricts SSN transmission and printing; a notification-style summary pointing to an authenticated channel is the minimal-risk pattern.

### Inferences
Concrete patterns for the target agent (inference):
- Verification as a tool, not a prompt: the LLM gathers the claimed identifiers and passes them to a verify_identity tool; the tool normalizes, compares server-side (ideally against hashed values) and returns only {verified, attempts_remaining}. The model never sees reference values, never sees a full SSN (ask only for the last 4; refuse and discard a full SSN if typed), and claim-data tools are gated on a server-side session flag, not on the model's belief that the user is verified.
- Pseudonymize prompts: after verification, refer to the person by a session token or masked claim reference; put only the claim facts needed for the answer (status, dates, amounts, outstanding items) in context, never DOB, SSN-4, phone or email.
- Redact at ingestion: users type identifiers in free text, so slot-level obfuscation is not enough. Run detection and redaction (e.g. Presidio plus regexes for SSN-4, DOB and phone formats) before anything reaches logs, analytics or LLM tracing, and keep raw verification inputs out of transcripts entirely; store only pass/fail per attempt in a restricted audit log.
- Disclosure ledger: an append-only record of timestamp, session ID, verified subject, requester role (policyholder or representative), consent basis and consent ID, data categories disclosed, and channel (chat or email). This supports HIPAA audit controls and claim-file reconstruction without retaining raw transcripts.
- Retention tiers: (a) material claim communications in the claim file for the state minimum (e.g. Virginia: current year plus 3 prior calendar years) and, for health plans, HIPAA documentation for 6 years where documentation is required; (b) raw transcripts redacted with a short TTL; (c) LLM-provider retention minimized (ZDR where the BAA allows it).
- Email summary: send only to the policyholder's email already on file, never to an address typed into the chat and not to the representative unless his consent scope covers it and his address is verified. Content: masked claim reference (last 4 characters), status, next steps, outstanding documents, how to reach a human, and a note that this was an automated assistant. Exclude any SSN digits, DOB, full policy or claim numbers, medical details and bank or payment details; prefer a link to an authenticated portal for anything detailed. If a health plan sends PHI by unencrypted email at the member's request, give the HHS-style warning and get confirmation first. Log the send in the disclosure ledger.

### Gaps
- Massachusetts 201 CMR 17.04(3) (encrypting personal information sent across public networks) could not be quoted; mass.gov returned no regulation text.
- NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law (#668) audit-trail and third-party-provider provisions were not retrieved.
- No authoritative source defines "safe content" for insurance claim summary emails; the list above is inference.
- Amazon Connect Contact Lens redaction, Azure AI Language PII detection and Twilio Verify defaults were not retrieved; Presidio's reversible (decrypt) operator was not confirmed in the fetched text.

## 7. Examples from deployed insurer, bank and health-plan chatbots: what is allowed before vs after authentication

### Takeaway
The largest banks and health plans put account- and claim-specific assistants behind an existing authenticated session (app or portal login) instead of verifying users inside the chat; family access runs through portal proxy features or authorization forms. Insurers increasingly use LLMs to draft claim communications with human review. Public primary detail on in-chat KBA by US insurers was not found.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTICE] Bank of America Erica: "Erica is exclusively available in the Mobile Banking app"; "You will need to be authenticated through the Mobile app to be able to use Erica"; interactions are "protected by the same industry-leading privacy and security features as the Mobile app and Online Banking." — [Bank of America: Erica](https://info.bankofamerica.com/en/digital-banking/erica.html)
- [PRACTICE] UnitedHealthcare "Avery" generative-AI companion (announced Mar 26, 2026): helps with claim approval status, explanations of benefits, cost estimates and plan balances, ID cards and provider search; eligible members use it through the UnitedHealthcare app or myuhc.com; initially about 6.5 million employer-plan members and 160,000 Medicare Advantage members, expanding to 20.5 million. — [UnitedHealth Group press release, Mar 26, 2026](https://www.unitedhealthgroup.com/newsroom/2026/2026-03-26-uhc-introduces-ai-companion-empowering-people-with-simpler-navigation-personal-experience.html); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/unitedhealthcare-launches-avery-generative-ai-companion-members)
- [PRACTICE] Elevance Health Virtual Assistant (Dec 5, 2025): available in the Sydney Health app and on affiliated plan websites for checking a claim, paying a bill or finding care; about 22 million commercial members, Medicare expansion planned for 2026. — [Elevance Health newsroom](https://www.elevancehealth.com/newsroom/elevance-health-expands-virtual-assistant-to-help-members-navigate-healthcare-with-confidence)
- [PRACTICE] Kaiser Permanente proxy access ("Act for a Family Member") for caregivers, with DPOA/guardianship required for adults lacking capacity (section 5). — [Kaiser](https://healthy.kaiserpermanente.org/northern-california/support/act-for-a-family-member)
- [PRACTICE] Allstate LLM-drafted claimant emails with rep review (section 6). — [The Register, Feb 13, 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/02/13/allstate_insurance_ai_rep/)
- [SECONDARY, dated] Lemonade's chatbot "Maya" handles sign-up and its claims bot resolves about 30% of claims without human intervention; GEICO's virtual assistant handles policy questions and claim updates; Progressive's "Flo" chatbot can help file claims and move payment dates. — [Emerj](https://emerj.com/chatbots-insurance-progessive-allstate-geico/)

### Inferences
- Dominant pattern: authenticate with the platform's strongest existing credential (app login, biometrics, MFA) before the assistant can read account data. Where an unauthenticated web chat is unavoidable, offer "log in to continue" or a one-time code to an on-file contact as the primary path and use KBA only as a fallback for low-risk read-only answers.
- Representative access in production systems is handled through portal proxy roles (Kaiser) or authorization forms (CMS-10106), not ad hoc consent inside a chat; a claims bot can mirror this by checking an authorization-on-file flag and by sending the policyholder a consent request through an authenticated channel.

### Gaps
- Capital One Eno, GEICO, Progressive, Lemonade and Allstate 2025-2026 chat authentication specifics (what is answered before vs after login, in-chat verification steps) were not found in primary sources; the Emerj summary is older secondary material.
- No public statement was found from these companies on transcript retention or redaction practice for their assistants.
